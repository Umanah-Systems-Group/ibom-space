Four parts, sold together or alone

An electric Hall thruster that runs on iodine, sold as parts to any spacecraft maker. It is also the engine inside our tug.

Iodine sits in the tank as a solid. It needs no high-pressure tank, it can wait fuelled on the ground for years, and it packs far more propellant into the same volume than xenon. Every part is designed, simulated and checked in Easley before it is built.

How Igwe works

  1. 01

    Solid fuel

    Iodine is stored as a solid, at low pressure. It launches safely and can sit fuelled on the shelf for years.

  2. 02

    Gentle heat

    Heaters warm the tank until the iodine turns to gas, and a valve meters it to the thruster in any orientation.

  3. 03

    Ionise and accelerate

    Inside the Hall thruster a magnetic field traps electrons that ionise the gas, and an electric field throws the ions out at high speed.

  4. 04

    Steer the push

    A two-axis gimbal points the thrust through the spacecraft’s centre of mass, so the push goes where it should.
